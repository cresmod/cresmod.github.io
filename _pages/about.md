Our research group focuses on investigating the cryosphere—the frozen component of the Earth system—which plays a vital role in regulating global climate and affecting societal well-being. As a sensitive indicator of climate change, the cryosphere has profound impacts on sea level rise, water resources, extreme weather events, and biodiversity. We aim to understand the past, present, and future evolution of the cryosphere and its interactions with both Earth and human systems. Positioned at the intersection of cryospheric science, remote sensing, geophysical modeling, and data science, our research primarily focuses on the Greenland and Antarctic Ice Sheets—critical components of the global cryosphere whose changes have far-reaching implications for sea level rise and climate variability. Beyond the polar regions, we actively collaborate on studies of high-altitude cryospheric systems, including the Himalayan glaciers, which are essential for water resources in densely populated areas. Additionally, our work extends to monitoring and understanding changes in water bodies at local and global scales, addressing critical environmental challenges.
